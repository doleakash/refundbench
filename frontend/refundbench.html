The Product Story: Automated Refund Decision System

1. Introduction

Imagine a busy marketplace where refund requests arrive every day. Cancellations, late deliveries, missing items, damaged goods, and vendor issues all require a decision.

For operations teams, every refund is a decision point: approve an unjustified refund and lose revenue, or reject a legitimate claim and risk customer trust.

This product uses evidence, policy, and an AI agent workflow to make refund decisions more consistent and observable.

2. The Persona

Role: Operations Manager

Mindset: Efficiency-focused while remaining customer-centric.

Pain: Agents must inspect order details, delivery records, customer claims, and refund policy across multiple sources. Manual review takes time and can lead to inconsistent decisions.

Goal: A command center that summarizes operational health and helps automate straightforward decisions while surfacing ambiguous cases for human review.

3. The Problem

  • Operational cost: Manual investigation consumes support-team time.
  • Decision quality: Inconsistent evidence assessment can lead to incorrect approvals or rejections.
  • Customer experience: Slow or incorrect resolutions reduce customer trust.
  • Visibility: Teams need to understand automation, escalation, latency, and decision outcomes.

4. Proposed Solution

RefundBench evaluates an agent-assisted refund workflow against a set of cases. The dashboard presents saved benchmark results, decision quality, latency, case outcomes, and recommended operational follow-ups.

Product principle
Automate only when evidence and policy support a confident decision. Ask for clarification or escalate when evidence is missing, conflicting, or ambiguous.

5. Success Metrics

  • Automation and escalation rates
  • Incorrect refund decisions, with the evaluated denominator clearly stated
  • Resolution latency (P50/P95)
  • Human-review workload estimates
  • Customer satisfaction, clearly distinguished from simulated benchmark proxies
How to interpret CSAT
RefundBench reports a simulated 1–5 CSAT score as a benchmark evaluation metric. It is derived from rubric-based proxies for decision correctness, fairness, completeness, and response clarity, then summarized across scored cases. It is not a survey, live customer feedback, or evidence of production satisfaction. Cases without sufficient evaluation data remain unscored.

6. Scope and Limitations

The current dashboard is a benchmark view, not proof of production performance. Estimates and simulated metrics must be labeled. Historical trends should appear only when actual dated runs are available.

Terminal classification runs after orchestration: cancelled/in-transit order → DEFLECTED; explicit escalation conditions → ESCALATED; otherwise → RESOLVED. Execution exceptions remain failed runs, not business outcomes.

Human review

Escalation signals route a case for review. Policy escalation does not itself guarantee the terminal workflow outcome is ESCALATED in the current classifier.

Execution failure

Uncaught errors and exhausted failures remain execution failures; they are kept distinct from RESOLVED, DEFLECTED, and ESCALATED.
